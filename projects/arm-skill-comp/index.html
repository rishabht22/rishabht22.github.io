ArmSkillComp

Goal-conditioned composition of robot manipulation skills

UC Berkeley · ICON Lab · Berkeley AI Research

MuJoCo workcell

I built a MuJoCo workcell with a Panda arm, an optical reader, and a rotary fixture with a mechanical interlock. I trained a PyTorch policy to decide which manipulation skill to run on which carrier. Each goal specifies which carriers to inspect, how many times to index them, and where to leave them. It can also require inspecting one carrier before indexing another.

Closed-loop skill selection

At each skill boundary, the system reads carrier locations, inspection flags, index counts, and the enclosure state from MuJoCo. It builds a 34-dimensional feature vector for every skill–carrier pair, encoding the candidate skill, the carrier’s observed and desired state, its role in the ordering constraint, and shared fixture occupancy.

A shared MLP with three 64-unit SiLU layers scores all 48 candidates using 10,625 parameters. With st as the observed state, g as the goal, and ϕ as the candidate features, the executed skill is the highest-scoring pair.

at= arg maxa∈𝒜 fθ(ϕ(st,g,a))

All candidates compete at inference time without a planner-provided action mask. The simulator checks preconditions and stops the rollout on a failed skill.

Training from a symbolic planner

I used breadth-first search over discrete skill effects to label every action that starts a shortest path to the goal. The planner only permits an index operation when the enclosure is latched and any required inspection has happened.

Let dg(s) be the fewest skills needed to reach goal g from state s, and Tg(s, a) the predicted state after a valid skill a. A skill is optimal if it reduces the remaining path by exactly one step.

dg(Tg(s,a)) =dg(s)−1

Several skills can satisfy that condition at the same state. The ranking term puts probability mass on the full set of optimal actions 𝒜*. With za as the network’s raw score for candidate a, the loss is the negative log of their combined softmax probability.

ℒrank= log∑a∈𝒜eza − log∑a∈𝒜∗eza

The training objective averages this term across the batch and adds a binary term weighted by 0.2. It averages softplus(−za) over optimal choices and softplus(za) over the other choices, then adds the two means.

Benchmark results by task condition

I compared a learned checkpoint, a planner that replans after each skill, and a fixed plan generated from an all-feed state. All three ran from the same 1,024 seeded resets in MuJoCo. On partial starts, the fixture was already loaded and its enclosure was closed or latched. The policy completed all 256 cases. The fixed plan hit a failed precondition in every case.

Carrier seating failures

The transfer controller uses the measured grasp transform to align the carrier with the slot. A successful seat requires position error below 3.8 mm, rotation error below 0.14 rad (about 8°), and contact with the slot floor. The controller can make one guarded recentering pass before releasing the carrier.

The learned checkpoint made zero invalid skill requests in this benchmark. All three failures occurred while seating a carrier in its feed slot. In one replay, the policy picked a valid reseat action, but the carrier hit the slot wall with about 9.2° of rotation error.
