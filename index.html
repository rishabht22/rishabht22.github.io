ArmSkillComp

A small learned policy that decides which manipulation skill to run next, and on which carrier, from the current state and the goal. It learns from a symbolic planner’s labels and re-scores after every skill, so it recovers from starts that a fixed plan can’t handle.

I got into robotics the competitive way: nearly six years on Innov8rz, a FIRST Tech Challenge team, eventually as its software captain. That’s where I learned that code only counts once it works on the actual robot.

These days I’m studying EECS at UC Berkeley as a Regents’ and Chancellor’s Scholar, and most of my time outside class goes to research. At ICON Lab I work on getting robots to coordinate when each one can only see its own surroundings, ideally by reusing skills they already have instead of training everything from scratch. With the Berkeley Speech Group, I’m working on something very different: teaching robotic faces the small cues that make a conversation feel natural.

Before Berkeley, I spent time on autonomous drones at Penn’s GRASP Lab and on self-driving perception at UC Santa Cruz. I like problems where the math has to survive contact with real hardware.

When I’m not doing any of that, I’m probably playing soccer, hunting for a new place to eat, out on a hike, or taking photos.
